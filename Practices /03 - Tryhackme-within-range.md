# Within Range: Digital Privacy & Phishing Awareness

**Platform:** TryHackMe  
**Category:** Cybersecurity Awareness / Digital Privacy  
**Status:** Completed

## Overview

This project documents a practical cybersecurity exercise on TryHackMe called *Within Range*.

The exercise explores how information about an individual's device and digital environment can potentially be exposed through seemingly ordinary interactions, as well as how attackers may use social engineering techniques to deceive users.

Through this exercise, I explored device information exposure, suspicious security notifications, and the identification of phishing indicators in fraudulent emails.

## Objectives

- Understand how personal device information can be exposed.
- Identify potential privacy risks associated with digital interactions.
- Recognise suspicious security notifications.
- Analyse email sender addresses and suspicious URLs.
- Identify common social engineering and phishing techniques.

## Practical Activities & Observations

### 1. Understanding Digital Exposure

Explored how information about a nearby user's device could potentially be collected without direct physical access to their phone.

The exercise presented exposed device information, including:

- Device type
- Operating system
- Browser
- Battery level
- Language settings
- Timezone
- Approximate location

**Key observation:** Seemingly harmless digital interactions can reveal information that users may not realise they are sharing.

### 2. Analysing Suspicious Security Notifications

Examined a simulated account security notification received shortly after the device-scanning scenario.

The notification claimed that an unfamiliar login had been detected and prompted immediate action.

**Key observation:** Unexpected security notifications can be used as an entry point for social engineering attacks, particularly when they create fear or urgency.

### 3. Identifying Phishing Indicators

Analysed a simulated phishing email containing several suspicious elements.

| Indicator | Observation |
|---|---|
| Sender address | Unfamiliar domain that did not establish a legitimate company identity |
| Suspicious URL | Link directed to an unrelated verification domain |
| Urgency | Threatened account suspension within 30 minutes |

**Key observation:** Familiar branding and convincing messages do not guarantee that an email is legitimate. The sender's domain, destination URL and message context should always be verified.

## Key Takeaways

Through this exercise, I learned the importance of:

- Being aware of the information exposed through digital interactions.
- Verifying email sender addresses before trusting security notifications.
- Inspecting URLs before clicking suspicious links.
- Recognising urgency and fear-based messaging as common social engineering techniques.
- Maintaining good digital privacy and security practices.
